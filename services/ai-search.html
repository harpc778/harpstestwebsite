What AI says about you

What does AI say about your business?

An AI answer is a shortlist your buyer didn't have to build themselves. If you're not on it, or you're on it for the wrong reasons, you won't see the lost enquiry in your analytics.

For every prompt I test, I look at three things:

Are you there?

Does your brand appear, and where does it sit against competitors?

How are you described?

Are your services, locations, pricing and audience right, or is the answer working from old information?

Who shaped the answer?

Which pages, directories, reviews, forums and videos the AI cited or leaned on.

Redacted example · Personal injury firmThree provinces
Prompt
Best personal injury lawyer in [city] for a car accident
Client
A personal injury firm with offices in three provinces
What we found
  1. Directories outranked the firm in AI answers.AI tools cited legal directories and review sites instead of the firm's own pages.
    High
  2. Competitors covered topics the firm didn't.Competing firms answered common injury and claims questions the firm's site didn't, so AI pulled from their pages instead.
    High
  3. Location pages were too thin to be cited.City and practice-area pages followed the same template with little unique local detail, which gave AI no reason to quote them.
    Medium
What we did next
Set up tracking across all three provincial sites, analyzed the competitors the firm was losing to, and refreshed the FAQ and blog content those answers were drawing on.

My process

How I find the work worth doing

  1. 01
    Test the questions buyers actually ask.

    Not thousands of rewordings of the same prompt. A focused set of questions a real customer asks before they buy, run across the platforms that matter for your market: ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, Gemini, Perplexity, Copilot.

  2. 02
    Look behind the answer.

    I inspect the citations and, where it's visible, the query fan-out: the searches the AI runs before it writes its answer. That shows you which pages and sources it trusts.

  3. 03
    Find the gaps on your own site.

    Pages that don't exist, pages that don't explain who you serve, and technical issues that stop your content being found in the first place.

  4. 04
    Find the gaps everywhere else.

    Third-party coverage, review platforms, directory profiles, Reddit and YouTube. Often the fix isn't on your website at all.

  5. 05
    Prioritize.

    You get a short list of what to fix first, tied to the questions most likely to bring in business.

Measurement

How we measure progress

Citations are interesting. Revenue is the point. I track both, and I'm clear about which is which.

  • Visibility signals: mentions, recommendations, accuracy and cited sources, tracked across a consistent set of prompts so month-to-month changes mean something.
  • Business signals: AI referral traffic, enquiries and self-reported attribution.

Self-reported attribution matters more than most teams expect. Add “AI tool” to your “How did you hear about us?” field, then ask what they searched. On one B2B software project, direct AI referral traffic was only a small part of what we were seeing. Asking buyers filled in the rest: you learn that someone asked for financial reporting software with a specific requirement, saw the product recommended, and later came back through branded search. Referral data alone would never show that.
