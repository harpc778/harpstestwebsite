B2B SEO & AI Search

I help B2B companies get found for the problems they solve and give buyers enough information to work out if they're a fit.

Talk about B2B search growth →
Buyer searches

Find the searches that can lead to business

B2B buyers rarely search for your category and stop there. They add the requirement that rules most vendors out.

Take financial reporting software. “Financial reporting software” is a big, competitive term. “Financial reporting software for multi-entity businesses” is smaller, but the person searching has a specific problem: consolidating results across several companies. If your product handles that well, that search is worth more than the big one, and it deserves a page that answers it properly.

I map the requirements your buyers bring (company size, industry, integrations, compliance, deployment) to the pages you have and the ones you're missing, then prioritize by buyer fit rather than search volume.

Pages that sell

Improve the pages buyers and sales teams use

The pages closest to a demo request do most of the work in B2B search, and they're often the least maintained. I work on:

Product and feature pages

What it does, explained in the words buyers use.

Solution and use-case pages

The problem it solves and how.

Industry pages

Why it fits a specific sector, with the details that sector cares about.

Integration pages

What it connects to and how, often the deciding factor late in a deal.

Bottom-of-funnel content

Pages built around the high-intent searches buyers make when they're shortlisting vendors, answering the questions that come up right before a demo.

I work with sales and product marketing to find the questions that keep coming up on calls. If prospects ask the same thing on every demo, the answer belongs on the website.

AI visibility

See how AI describes and recommends your business

Ask an AI tool for “the best CRM” and you'll often get the best for enterprise, the best for small businesses, the best for a specific industry. You want AI tools to understand which of those buckets you belong in.

I test prompts built around real buyer requirements, industries and company sizes, then check:

  • Whether AI tools recommend you for the segments you actually serve
  • Whether they describe your positioning correctly, or are still working from an old version of it
  • Which sources inform that positioning: your site, review platforms like G2 and Capterra, analyst coverage, industry articles, forums
Pipeline

Connect search to demos and pipeline

Traffic is useful. Qualified demos, trials and opportunities are what the board asks about. I set up reporting that follows search through to pipeline:

  • Analytics: organic and AI referral sessions to the pages that drive conversions
  • CRM data: which demos and opportunities started from organic or AI search
  • Self-reported attribution: an “AI tool” option on your forms, plus a question about what they searched, to catch the buyers who discover you in ChatGPT and come back later through branded search
